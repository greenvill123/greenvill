RBIs Repo Rate Outlook & How It Affects Your Home Loan EMI

Gopikrishnan M
Principal Director, Greenvill Associates Trichy | Published: August 2026

For home loan borrowers across India and specifically home buyers in Trichy, few macroeconomic metrics carry as much direct financial impact as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Repo Rate. Since October 2019, RBI mandated that all commercial banks link their floating rate home loans directly to an external benchmark, which for most banks is the RBI Repo Rate—giving birth to Repo Rate Linked Lending Rates (RLLR).

1. What is RLLR and How Does It Work?

Under RLLR, your home loan interest rate is directly tied to the central bank's key policy rate. The final interest rate charged to you consists of two main components:

Home Loan Interest Rate = Current RBI Repo Rate + Bank Spread / Margin

For instance, if the current RBI Repo Rate is 6.50% and your bank's operating margin is 1.85%, your effective floating home loan interest rate stands at 8.35% p.a.

2. How Repo Rate Hikes vs Cuts Impact Your EMI

Whenever the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) adjusts the Repo Rate, banks are legally mandated to pass on the rate adjustment to existing floating-rate borrowers within 90 days. Here is how changes manifest:

  • When Repo Rate Increases: Your bank automatically increases your interest rate. In most cases, banks increase your total loan tenure while keeping the monthly EMI amount constant, unless your tenure reaches the maximum cap (e.g. 30 years).
  • When Repo Rate Decreases: Your interest rate drops, resulting in reduced total interest payable over the loan duration or immediate reduction in monthly EMI.

3. Strategic Actions for Borrowers in 2026

If your existing home loan interest rate is above 10.5% (common for older MCLR or Base Rate loans sanctioned before 2019), you are paying lakhs of rupees in avoidable interest costs. Switching to an RLLR-linked home loan through a Balance Transfer can instantly reduce your interest rate down to 8.35% p.a.
